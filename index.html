The Best Curried Scrambled Eggs Recipe

Curried Scrambled Eggs are a versatile breakfast dish that transforms plain eggs with aromatic spices and is typically served with toast or flatbread.

This recipe makes 1 serving and takes approximately ten minutes to prepare. (Just double or triple the ingredients for multiple servings.)

Ingredients

  • 3 large eggs
  • 1 medium onion (red or white), thinly sliced
  • 2 cloves of garlic, finely diced
  • 1 tsp of curry powder
  • 1 cup baby spinach
  • 1 tbsp olive oil or coconut oil
  • 1 half tsp of salt
  • 1 tbsp lemon juice
  • Chilli flakes, for heat

Instructions

  1. Prepare your serving plate by lining it with a generous bed of fresh baby spinach.
  2. Whisk the eggs thoroughly in a bowl.
  3. Heat olive oil in a medium pan over medium-high heat.
  4. Add the onion and salt and cook for about 4 min until soft and golden brown.
  5. Add the garlic and curry powder and stir for 45 seconds.
  6. Pour the egg into the mixture and stir gently and continuously for about 30 seconds while scraping the bottom of the pan and folding until just set but still glossy and slightly creamy.
  7. Immediatly remove the eggs onto a plate lined with a bed of fresh baby spinach.
  8. Drizzled with the fresh lemon juice.
  9. Top with chilli flakes to your desired heat level!

A quick historical note about the origins of curry powder:

Curry powder is a British invention, not a traditional Indian spice blend. It was created by British colonizers in the 18th century (first commercially sold in 1784) to replicate the complex flavors of Indian cuisine for European cooks who lacked access to fresh ingredients or the knowledge to mix spices from scratch.

Origin:

The blend was adapted from Indian garam masala but simplified into a single, all-purpose powder.

Key Ingredients:

It typically consists of turmeric, coriander, cumin, fenugreek, mustard, and black pepper.

Global Spread:

British traders introduced the powder to Japan in the 19th century, where it evolved into Japanese curry, and it later spread to the Caribbean, Africa, and Southeast Asia through colonial trade and indentured labor migration.

Distinctive Ingredients:

While the individual spices are native to the Indian subcontinent, the pre-mixed "curry powder" itself is an Anglo-Indian creation and is not used in authentic traditional Indian cooking.
